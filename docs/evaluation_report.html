2.1Intent Router

Dataset — 16 hand-written Telugu farmer questions (single-intent, multi-intent, no-intent/greeting), with ground-truth {wants_disease, wants_weather, wants_price, wants_soil} labels.
Method — live calls to route_intent(), exact-match scoring across all 4 fields.

Result — 81.8% (held-out), not 87.5% (full set), is the number to cite

14/16 correct, 87.5% on the full set, re-run live today — matches the original Phase 2.2 measurement exactly. But 5 of these 16 questions are verbatim few-shot examples inside the router's own SYSTEM_PROMPT (see External validity below). Recomputing on only the 11 genuinely held-out questions gives 9/11, 81.8% — the more honest real-world estimate. This is worse news than 87.5% suggests, not just a smaller, noisier sample: both of the full set's 2 failures (#12, #14) fall inside the 11 held-out questions, so neither was "propped up" by the few-shot-exposed subset scoring artificially well — removing that subset concentrates the same 2 errors onto a smaller base rather than diluting them.

Failure modes (n=1 each, illustrative): #12 under-routed a broad "full advice" request (missed weather/price); #14 over-routed a pesticide-availability question as disease-related.

Headline limitation — compound-intent questions are where this breaks

Question #12 ("నా టమాటా పంట గురించి పూర్తి సలహా కావాలి" / "I want full advice on my tomato crop") is the only question in the set whose ground truth expects three intents simultaneously (wants_disease=wants_weather=wants_price=True) — and it's exactly the one the router does worst on, recovering only 1 of the 3 expected labels. The model appears able to hold one clear intent judgment reliably, but its ability to affirm multiple, simultaneous intents degrades on a maximal, "tell me everything" style question — precisely the kind of open-ended request a real farmer is likely to actually ask. A farmer asking that exact question today would silently lose the weather and price portions of the answer they expected, with no error or partial-answer indication surfaced anywhere in the pipeline. Flagged as a concrete direction for future work (e.g. a routing prompt that enumerates each intent as an independent check), not applied as a fix here — observation only, orchestrator/intent_router.py untouched.

The gap, diagnosed

A 71.5-point drop is textbook domain shift, and came with a specific failure mode: the model over-predicted Tomato_Late_blight for 49% of test images (true rate: 12%) — an attractor-class bias, not just noise.

Is this one model's fluke, or a structural property of the domain gap? Cross-architecture convergence

The single-checkpoint baseline above (28.2%, 85-image test-only subset) could, on its own, be one model's idiosyncrasy. It isn't: two more independently-trained EfficientNetB0 checkpoints — a flat 13-class baseline, and a separately-trained flat "v2" checkpoint from a fresh Colab session — were evaluated the same way, and all three converge on three independent signals, not accuracy alone.

SignalFinding
1. Accuracy bandAll 3 checkpoints' zero-shot PlantDoc accuracy (full train+test, n=965–968) lands in a 2-point band: 22.00% (flat) / 23.45% (hierarchical) / 21.45% (v2) — mean 22.30%, stdev 1.03pp
2. Attractor-class (now a formal confusion matrix)Both checkpoints with saved per-image predictions independently default to Tomato_Late_blight/Tomato_Early_blight regardless of true label — hierarchical: Tomato_Late_blight predicted 522/967 times (54% of all predictions); v2: Tomato_Late_blight (425×) + Tomato_Early_blight (322×) dominate the same way. Hierarchical checkpoint: 80.2% recall but only 17.0% precision on Tomato_Late_blight — it calls almost everything blight
3. Calibration shapeExpected Calibration Error nearly identical across both checkpoints (0.4725 vs. 0.4606) — both reliability diagrams show the same shape: confidence bins above ~0.5 sit well below the diagonal, i.e. the model is systematically overconfident, not just inaccurate, and this replicates independently
Read together, not separately

These are not three separate findings — they are the same underlying phenomenon viewed at three resolutions of the same confusion matrix, replicated across three independently-trained models, two training environments, and two architectures. The evidence points to a decision boundary shaped almost entirely by PlantVillage's uniform lab backgrounds — a "blotchy texture on a leaf-shaped object, pick the nearest common label" heuristic — rather than lesion-specific morphology. This is strong evidence the ~22% real-world accuracy ceiling is a structural covariate-shift problem inherent to PlantVillage-only training data, not a fixable modeling error specific to one run, and it is this project's most scientifically interesting result. Stated plainly, without hedging: the Disease Agent, as currently trained, should not be presented as reliable for field deployment — this three-signal convergence is the evidence for that limitation, not a hidden weakness.

Full evidence: docs/evidence/metric1_zero_shot_convergence.json, docs/evidence/metric1_confusion_matrix_calibration_evidence.json, docs/evidence/model_lineage.md. Row 1 (flat baseline) is not included in the confusion-matrix/ECE work specifically — no per-image predictions exist for it in this repo; the 3-checkpoint accuracy-band convergence above still covers all three.

A specific, useful finding

Tomato_healthy has precision 1.0, recall 0.375 — the model almost never mislabels a diseased leaf as healthy (no false "all clear"), but does mislabel some healthy leaves as diseased (false alarms). For a farm advisory tool this is the safer direction to be wrong in.

Architecture ablation

Hierarchical (crop → per-crop disease) scored 99.70% PlantVillage validation accuracy vs. a flat 13-class classifier's 99.72% — statistically indistinguishable on the training distribution. The hierarchical architecture was kept for cleaner separation of crop-ID error from disease-ID error, visible in the per-class table above, not for a raw accuracy gain.

2.6Intent Router / TTS memory-collision fix — cost-benefit

Root cause — Ollama keeps a model resident in RAM for 5 minutes post-call by default; on an 8GB machine, the 7B Intent Router model and TTS collided for memory, producing the 191s+ stalls in §2.4.
Fix — force-unload the Intent Router model immediately after each use.

Original 3-trial spot check

5.6s / 12.1s / 10.7s per call, vs. ~3.6s warm — roughly a 2–3× per-question latency cost, traded for eliminating the ~191s collision entirely.

Formal 8-trial re-measurement — higher, reported honestly

mean 13.58s · std 3.18s · min 11.45s · max 20.99s (n=8) — notably higher than the original spot check. Not reconciled or picked-favorably: this run's 8 router trials immediately followed 8 TTS trials in the same process (§2.4), so residual memory pressure from those calls is a plausible unisolated confound; ordinary session-to-session system variance is a second, equally plausible explanation. What both measurements agree on doesn't change: the fix costs single-digit-to-low-double-digit seconds, several orders of magnitude below the ~191s collision it prevents.

Full-request, per-stage latency

6 real end-to-end /api/ask HTTP trials, covering different request shapes (weather-only, price-only, disease-with-photo, multi-agent, voice). Per external review (2026-09-13): median is now reported alongside mean, since the mean-vs-median gap is itself informative about skew.

2.7End-to-end: text-only pipeline characterization

Every metric above is per-component. This runs the full, live pipeline across the same 16 questions and captures what kind of answer a farmer actually gets — not just crash/no-crash (100% reliable, reproduced today).

Result

Substantive-answer rate: 43.8% (7/16) — the rest fall back to a generic "consult a local officer" sentence. Every fallback individually root-caused below, not left as a bare number.

CauseCountAssessment
No Soil Agent exists at all3Structural gap — a real Intent Router category with zero implementation
Disease intent, no photo attached4Expected — this test set is intentionally text-only
No intent detected (a greeting)1Expected — nothing to answer
Conflict Resolver rule-table gap1Genuine finding — see below
Precisely diagnosed bug

"Should I sell or hold, considering price and weather?" returns weather=rain_risk, price=sell_now, disease=None. The rule table had an entry for (None, rain_risk, hold) but none for (None, rain_risk, sell_now) — an asymmetric gap, not a systemic flaw. Only 1 of 16 (6.25%) was an actual bug-adjacent finding; the rest is correct, expected behavior for this test's conditions. Fixed 2026-09-13 (weather and price agree here, so it resolves as a synergy case, not a conflict) and added as a permanent regression test — see §2.0's now-13/13 conflict-resolver result.

2.8End-to-end: disease-with-photo accuracy

Every Disease Agent number in §2.5 measures the classifier in isolation. This measures the full pipeline's final spoken-answer text against real PlantDoc photos with known ground truth.

Method — 30 real PlantDoc test images (3 per class, 10 classes), full pipeline call per image. Correctness requires the final Telugu answer to name both the correct crop and disease — an earlier draft matched on disease name alone and overcounted, since several Telugu labels repeat across crops (Tomato_Early_blight and Potato_Early_blight both render as "ఎర్లీ బ్లైట్"); fixed before reporting.

Result

36.7% end-to-end (11/30) — identical to component-level accuracy on the same 30 images. Wiring integrity: 100% — every correct classifier prediction survived intact through Conflict Resolver and Phrasing Templates. The end-to-end ceiling is set entirely by the Disease Agent's own accuracy, not by anything downstream.

A repeat run before the crop+disease fix measured 33.3% (10/30) on identical images/weights — a 1-image difference traced to Apple MPS backend's known minor floating-point nondeterminism, flagged rather than silently using whichever run looked better.

2.9Voice vs. text ablation preliminary, n=16, single speaker

Does going through ASR change what the pipeline does, compared to typing the exact same question? Each of the 16 questions run through the real, unmocked pipeline twice — once via speaker1's recorded WAV through the exact production ASR call path, once as the reference text typed directly. Both paths use live, unmocked agent calls. Stage-by-stage comparison (ASR transcript, intent router, agents fired, final answer), not just final-answer pass/fail, so an ASR mishearing and a downstream difference stay distinguishable rather than collapsed into one number.

Result

Intent-router output matched 15/16; agents-fired matched 15/16. Of the 2 apparent final-answer mismatches, one was investigated and ruled out as a live Price API fluctuation unrelated to voice vs. text — leaving exactly one genuine voice-vs-text pipeline finding out of 16 questions.

Headline finding (question 8) — ASR transliteration of a technical term changed intent classification

"నేల pH ఎంత ఉండాలి టమాటా కోసం?" (What soil pH is needed for tomato?): ASR transcribed "pH" as its Telugu transliteration "పీహెచ్" instead of the reference text's Latin-script "pH." That single spelling difference flipped the LLM intent router's classification — the Latin-script version correctly routed to wants_soil=True; the Telugu-transliterated version routed instead to wants_price=True, so a farmer asking about soil pH by voice would have received a tomato price quote instead. This is a structural finding, not an ASR accuracy defect — the transcription was arguably more natural Telugu than the reference text, and that naturalness is exactly what caused the behavior change. It demonstrates that domain-specific technical terms (soil pH, NPK, pesticide/disease names) crossing the ASR-to-LLM boundary can silently change system behavior in ways a text-only test suite would never catch — the actual reason to run a voice-vs-text ablation at all. Flagged as a concrete direction for future work (e.g. normalizing known technical terms before intent routing), not applied as a fix here — observation only, orchestrator/pipeline.py untouched.

One apparent mismatch investigated and ruled out (question 7, potato market price): same intents, same agents fired on both paths, but a different final price quote. Traced to the Price Agent, which hits data.gov.in live with no caching on every call — checked every price-agent call across the entire run and found 8 of 9 returned the identical value to 16 decimal places, including this question's own text-path call made 4 seconds later; only the voice-path call for this question differed. Confirmed via source read (not inference) that price_agent.py's code path cannot differ by invocation source at all — get_price_advice() never receives the question text. Settled, not provisional: a one-off live API fluctuation, not a voice-vs-text pipeline effect.

Preliminary — n=16, single speaker (speaker1) only, same caveat as §2.3's WER check at 1/4 speakers. Should be re-run against speakers 2–4 once their WER recordings are available, using the same reusable script. Full evidence: docs/evidence/metric4_voice_vs_text_ablation_evidence.json.

Honest reading — not a one-sided one

Farmer.Chat massively outscales this project on real-world deployment and user-study rigor — a genuine, uncontested strength of theirs. This comparison supports a narrower, verifiable claim instead: conflict arbitration between competing advice signals, and prospective confidence communication to the farmer, are explicitly engineered and independently tested in Krishi-Agent — and are not described as present in any of these three systems' own published accounts. That's the specific, evidence-backed novelty contribution here, not a claim of being "better overall," which the evaluation-scale gap above would not support.

Real-world plant disease classification

PlantDoc is an established benchmark specifically because it exposes the PlantVillage-to-field accuracy gap this project also measured. Published results vary widely by model scale and training data — large hybrid transformer models trained substantially on in-domain data report 95–99% on PlantDoc; smaller models trained mostly on PlantVillage report much lower numbers, consistent with the 28–40% range found here for a similarly-scoped small-data fine-tune.

4Limitations

  • Disease Agent real-world accuracy remains modest (37.65% end-to-end) for 8 of 10 disease classes with real data; genuinely reliable only for crop ID (82%) and Potato_healthy (94%).
  • Weather drought signal is a short-range proxy (5-day forecast uniformly dry+hot), not true multi-week drought detection.
  • The 0.7 disease confidence cutoff is a draft threshold — a weak but real separation was found (26.5% vs 19.9% accuracy above/below it), not rigorous calibration.
  • The Price Agent's ±10% sell/hold thresholds (SELL_THRESHOLD/HOLD_THRESHOLD) are unvalidated constants, the same category of gap the disease confidence cutoff was before that investigation. A descriptive check (2026-09-13) confirmed the thresholds are at least a non-degenerate operating point on the real 2002–2026 historical distribution (~34%/56%/10% sell/hold/neutral split for Telangana Tomato) — but this is descriptive only, not validation against real farmer outcomes. A rigorous outcome-based backtest was deliberately not attempted: it requires an assumed holding-horizon (tomatoes are perishable, so "wait N days" needs a specific N), and this project has no solid agronomic grounding for that assumption — held as explicit future work (§5), not a time-constraint shortcut.
  • The Price Agent's seasonal baseline is an arithmetic mean over a right-skewed price distribution, which mechanically biases it toward triggering hold far more often than sell_now — a distinct finding from the threshold-value question above, not a restatement of it. The same check found hold firing ~1.7× more often than sell_now across every state/commodity combination tested. Root cause, verified directly: raw Telangana Tomato modal price has skew 2.23 (mean ₹1,454 vs. median ₹1,000) — occasional price-spike days pull the mean well above what a "typical" day actually sees, so a typical day sits below that inflated baseline more often than above it, by construction. This is independent of whatever the "correct" ±10% value turns out to be — even a perfectly chosen threshold applied to a systematically skewed baseline would inherit this same asymmetry. Not fixed here — see §5, the specific proposed change (switch to a median baseline).
  • Intent Router latency tradeoff is accepted, not eliminated (§2.6).
  • Most evaluation sets are small (n=12–85) except the expanded Potato_healthy check (n=263) — flagged explicitly, not hidden.
  • No end-to-end human evaluation of final spoken answers exists yet — every number above is per-component or programmatically checked, not a holistic human judgment of correctness and helpfulness.
  • No user study with real farmers or Telugu speakers has been conducted.

  5. A proper literature review — §3 is a starting point.
  6. DoneFormal latency benchmarking — §2.4/§2.6 now report real n=8-trial mean/std/min/max with hardware spec, and it caught a genuine discrepancy worth investigating further (the Intent Router's 8-trial mean vs. the original 3-trial spot check).
  7. Price Agent threshold outcome validation. The descriptive distribution check (§4) confirms the ±10% thresholds aren't a degenerate operating point, but doesn't validate them against real farmer outcomes. A proper version needs an outcome-based backtest against an explicitly-justified holding-horizon assumption — tomato perishability makes this a real agronomic question, not just a modeling detail, and deserves domain input before being attempted rather than an arbitrarily chosen horizon. Data already available and sufficient (116K+/39K+ rows for Tomato/Potato, 2002–2026) — this is a scoping/domain-input gap, not a data-availability one.
  8. Switch the Price Agent's seasonal baseline from arithmetic mean to median. Specific, named fix for the mean-vs-skew asymmetry in §4 — _seasonal_baseline() currently returns .mean(); switching to .median() would make the baseline robust to the rare high-price spike days that pull it upward, and should materially reduce (though not by itself validate) the ~1.7× hold-over-sell_now asymmetry. Not a guarantee — the confidence-bucket and sell/hold thresholds were never chosen with a median baseline in mind and would need re-characterizing against it. Small, mechanical, low-risk change in isolation; not made in this pass because it changes live pipeline behavior and needs its own sign-off and before/after comparison, same discipline as every other pipeline.py change this evaluation phase.
